ACADEMIC PROFILE / 2026

Hey, fellow observer.

Welcome to my little corner of the universe!

I'm Sharad Shukla, a graduate student exploring the fascinating world of Theoretical High Energy Physics, where the smallest constituents of nature may hold clues to the deepest mysteries of our universe.

I'm fascinated by the interplay of mathematics and fundamental physics, where abstract structures reveal hidden patterns of nature, symmetries illuminate the underlying principles of reality, and unanswered questions invite us to venture deeper into the mysteries of the universe.

For me, physics is more than the study of what the universe is; it is an attempt to understand why it is the way it is. This page is a space to share that journey — through abstract ideas, fundamental principles, and the beautiful pursuit of understanding the cosmos, one question at a time.

Somewhere between an equation and a question lies a universe waiting to be understood.

And I am one more calculation away from understanding reality. Probably.

After all, the universe is vast, our understanding is incomplete, and there is always more to wonder about.

01 / FOCUS

Research interests

I'm interested in the theoretical frameworks we use to describe fundamental particles, interactions, and the structure of physical laws. Some of the broad areas I find compelling include:

01

Quantum Field Theory

Understanding fields, particles, interactions, and the mathematical language underlying modern particle physics.

02

Symmetries and Mathematical Physics

Exploring how symmetry principles, group structures, and invariance guide the formulation of physical theories.

03

Fundamental Interactions

Investigating the theoretical descriptions of elementary particles and the forces through which they interact.

04

Conceptual Foundations

Thinking about the assumptions, mathematical consistency, and conceptual questions that shape our picture of reality.

These are areas of interest, not a claim that I have conducted research in every topic listed.

02 / APPROACH

How I think about physics

I enjoy the process of moving between physical intuition and mathematical precision: starting with a question, identifying the relevant principles, and following the mathematics to see where it leads.

Equations are more than computational tools. They encode relationships, reveal constraints, and sometimes expose connections that are difficult to see in words alone.

I'm especially drawn to questions where a simple-looking principle has far-reaching consequences, or where a familiar theory raises a deeper question about its own foundations.
